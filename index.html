What the article argues

Three ideas in brief

1

Bigger, but lopsided

China–Central Asia trade hit a record US$106.3 billion in 2025, yet China exported roughly twice as much as it imported.

2

Connectivity cuts both ways

It can widen a country’s options or concentrate them around one partner. The evidence supports both possibilities.

3

Routes need diversification too

New transport routes strengthen strategic autonomy when paired with diversified finance, trading partners and exports.

The central question

Where does engagement lead?

Does greater connectivity give Central Asian states more choices, or more dependence?

Trade between China and the five Central Asian states has grown from US$460 million in 1992 to over US$70 billion in 2022. The blog uses Kazakhstan as its main case to ask whether that growth widens or narrows the region’s strategic options.

Its answer: the challenge is not whether to engage with China, but whether governments can develop enough viable alternatives to negotiate the terms of that engagement.
